### The Unfolding Glory of Jesus Christ in His Judgments, Mercy, and Grace

------

## Part 1: Living in the Light of Revelation

**Key idea:** Revelation was not given to frighten God's people but to strengthen them for the spiritual conflict Paul describes in Ephesians 6. The book equips believers with an eternal perspective, the certainty of Christ's victory, and hope in His return.

**Passages to Read:** Ephesians 6:10-20; Matthew 28:18-20; Revelation 21-22

**Main points:**

- The Christian life is lived inside a real spiritual conflict, one that is not ultimately against people or circumstances but against unseen opposition to God and His purposes (Ephesians 6:12).
- Revelation gives the church heaven's perspective on that conflict: it names the reality of demonic activity, suffering, and persecution, without minimizing the cost of following Jesus.
- Even so, Christ sovereignly reigns. Evil is real but not ultimate; Satan is active but not sovereign.
- Because Christ holds all authority, His church can carry out the Great Commission with confidence.

**Reflection questions:**

1. Where in your own life does the "armor of God" language (truth, righteousness, the gospel of peace, faith, salvation, the Word) feel most needed right now?
2. How does knowing the outcome of the conflict (Christ's victory) change how you face present struggles?
3. Revelation 5:5-6 pairs the Lion of Judah with the slain Lamb. What does it mean that the one who judges is also the one who was slain for sinners?

## Part 2: How to Read Revelation

### A. God's Character: The Foundation of Revelation

**Key idea:** Before interpreting Revelation's symbols and judgments, readers need to start with who God is. His wrath, justice, mercy, and grace are not competing qualities. All flow from His one, unchanging, holy love.

**Passages to have open:** Revelation 5:6; Revelation 1:3

**Main points:**

- The Lamb who was slain and the Lion who judges are the same Lord, not two different portraits in tension.
- Revelation's judgments are not arbitrary outbursts. They are the righteous response of a holy God who will not let evil have the final word.
- The right question when reading a symbol isn't only "what does this mean?" but "what does this show me about God, Christ, and the hope He has given His people?"

**Reflection questions:**

1. Have you ever thought of God's wrath and God's mercy as being in tension with each other? How does this section reframe that?
2. How might approaching Revelation with "what does this show me about God" as a guiding question change the way you read the more difficult chapters?

### B. The Blessing of Revelation

**Key idea:** Revelation opens with a promise of blessing (1:3) grounded not in human effort but in union with Christ. The Greek word *makarios* ("blessed") describes the favored, privileged condition of those who belong to God through Jesus, even in hard circumstances. This theme of blessing runs throughout the book in seven distinct declarations.

**Passages to have open:** Revelation 1:3; Ephesians 1:3-6; Ephesians 2:8-10; 2 Corinthians 5:21; James 1:2-4

**The Seven Blessings of Revelation:**

| Reference | What it blesses                                        |
| :-------- | :----------------------------------------------------- |
| 1:3       | Hearing and keeping God's Word                         |
| 14:13     | Dying in the Lord                                      |
| 16:15     | Staying spiritually alert                              |
| 19:9      | Being invited to the marriage supper of the Lamb       |
| 20:6      | Sharing in the first resurrection                      |
| 22:7      | Keeping the words of this prophecy                     |
| 22:14     | Having a right to the tree of life / entering the city |

**Main points:**

- The blessing is not earned; it rests entirely on grace received through faith in Christ.
- To "keep" Revelation's words means more than memorizing them. It means receiving its testimony about Jesus, worshiping God alone, resisting compromise with evil, remaining faithful under trial, and living in hope of Christ's return.
- Growth in Christ happens through ordinary means: hearing the Word, prayer, worship, and bearing witness, all while keeping our eyes fixed on Him (2 Corinthians 3:18; Hebrews 12:2).
- The book's climax (Revelation 22:1-5) is the promise that Christ's people will see His face and reign with Him forever.

**Reflection questions:**

1. Look through the seven blessings above. Which one speaks most directly to where you are right now?
2. The guide distinguishes "keeping" Revelation from simply reading or memorizing it. What would it look like practically to "keep" what you read this week?
3. Read 2 Corinthians 3:18. How does beholding Christ's glory (rather than sheer willpower) produce transformation, according to this section?

## Key Term

**Makarios** (Greek, translated "blessed") - describes the favored and privileged condition of those who belong to God through Christ, regardless of present circumstances. Not primarily an emotional state, but a status.

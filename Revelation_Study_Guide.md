# Revelation Study Guide: God's Judgment and Mercy

## Introduction: Understanding Revelation

Revelation identifies itself in its opening verse as "the revelation of Jesus Christ." The Greek word *apokalypsis* means to "uncover," "unveil," or "disclose" information that only God would know. Revelation is not merely a book about the end times—it is the unveiling of Jesus Christ and the ultimate triumph of everything God is and has promised.

At first glance, Revelation appears to present two contrasting portraits of Christ: the Lamb who was slain and the Lion who judges. Read carefully, however, it reveals one unified story: the story of a God whose judgment and mercy arise from the same source—**God's love**.

---

## Theme 1: God's Love as the Foundation

**Core Truth:** God's infinite love forms the foundation for all His actions toward humanity.

### Supporting Passages

**Old Testament:**
- Deuteronomy 7:7-8 — God's love for Israel is not based on their size or merit, but on His choice and covenant faithfulness
- Psalm 103:10-12 — God's compassion and mercy flow from His love for His people
- Jeremiah 31:3 — "I have loved you with an everlasting love"

**New Testament:**
- John 3:16 — "For God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son"
- 1 John 4:7-8 — "Dear friends, let us love one another, for love comes from God... God is love"
- 1 Corinthians 13:4-7 — The characteristics of love: patient, kind, protective, trusting, persevering

**Revelation:**
- Revelation 1:5-6 — Christ as the "faithful witness" who "loves us and has freed us from our sins"
- Revelation 3:19 — "Those whom I love I rebuke and discipline"

### Discussion Questions
1. How does understanding God's love as the foundation change the way you read Revelation?
2. What does it mean that God is love (not just that God acts lovingly)?

---

## Theme 2: God's Holiness and Justice

**Core Truth:** God's holiness explains why sin must be judged.

### Supporting Passages

**Old Testament:**
- Isaiah 6:1-5 — Isaiah's vision of God's holiness and his own unworthiness
- Leviticus 19:2 — "Be holy because I, the Lord your God, am holy"
- Psalm 99:1-3 — God's holiness and justice are interconnected

**New Testament:**
- Romans 3:25-26 — God's justice requires payment for sin; Christ's blood satisfies this requirement
- Hebrews 10:10-14 — Christ's sacrifice perfects all who are sanctified

**Revelation:**
- Revelation 4:8 — The seraphim cry out, "Holy, holy, holy is the Lord God Almighty"
- Revelation 15:4 — "Who will not fear you, Lord, and bring glory to your name? For you alone are holy"
- Revelation 20:11-15 — God's just judgment of all people according to their deeds

### Discussion Questions
1. How do God's holiness and His love work together rather than against each other?
2. Why is God's judgment necessary for His love to be complete?

---

## Theme 3: God's Wrath—Holy Opposition to Evil

**Core Truth:** God's wrath is not the opposite of love; it is the holy opposition of His infinite love to everything that contradicts His goodness and destroys what He loves.

### Supporting Passages

**Old Testament:**
- Exodus 34:6-7 — God is "slow to anger" but will not leave the guilty unpunished
- Psalm 7:11 — "God is a righteous judge, a God who expresses his wrath every day"
- Nahum 1:2-3 — God's jealousy and wrath against those who oppose Him

**New Testament:**
- Romans 1:18 — "The wrath of God is being revealed from heaven against all the godlessness and wickedness of people"
- 1 Thessalonians 5:9 — Believers are not appointed to experience God's wrath but salvation through Christ
- 1 Corinthians 13:6 — "Love finds no joy in unrighteousness but rejoices in the truth"

**Revelation:**
- Revelation 6:15-17 — People hide from "the wrath of the Lamb"
- Revelation 11:18 — God's wrath against those who destroy the earth
- Revelation 14:10-11 — The consequences of rejecting God's grace
- Revelation 19:11-16 — Christ as the righteous judge whose eyes are like blazing fire

### Discussion Questions
1. How does a parent's protective anger toward someone harming their child illustrate God's wrath?
2. Can God's wrath and God's love coexist? How?

---

## Theme 4: God's Mercy—Withholding Deserved Judgment

**Core Truth:** His mercy means He withholds the judgment sinners deserve.

### Supporting Passages

**Old Testament:**
- Psalm 103:10 — "He does not treat us as our sins deserve or repay us according to our iniquities"
- Lamentations 3:22-23 — God's compassion and mercies are new every morning
- Micah 7:18-19 — God delights in mercy and compassion

**New Testament:**
- Romans 3:23-24 — All have sinned, yet all are justified freely by His grace through redemption
- Ephesians 2:4-5 — "Because of his great love for us, God, who is rich in mercy, made us alive with Christ"
- Titus 3:5 — God saved us "because of his mercy, not because of righteous things we had done"

**Revelation:**
- Revelation 21:4 — God will wipe away all tears; there will be no more death, mourning, or pain
- Revelation 22:17 — "Let the one who is thirsty come; let the one who desires take the water of life freely"

### Discussion Questions
1. What is the difference between mercy and grace?
2. How does God's patience provide opportunities for repentance?

---

## Theme 5: God's Grace—Giving the Undeserved

**Core Truth:** His grace means He gives sinners what they could never earn—forgiveness, righteousness, and life in Christ.

### Supporting Passages

**Old Testament:**
- Genesis 6:8 — Noah "found favor [grace] in the eyes of the Lord"
- Psalm 84:11 — "The Lord bestows favor and honor; no good thing does he withhold"

**New Testament:**
- John 1:16-17 — "From the fullness of his grace we have all received grace in place of grace already given. For the law was given through Moses; grace and truth came through Jesus Christ"
- Ephesians 2:8-9 — "For it is by grace you have been saved, through faith—and this is not from yourselves, it is the gift of God"
- Romans 6:23 — "The wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord"
- John 3:16-21 — God's grace offers eternal life to all who believe

**Revelation:**
- Revelation 22:12-13 — Christ's reward is for those who have washed their robes in His blood
- Revelation 1:5-6 — Through Christ's blood, we are freed from sins and made "a kingdom and priests to serve our God"

### Discussion Questions
1. Why is grace sometimes harder to accept than judgment?
2. How does understanding grace change your daily Christian life?

## Theme 6: The Cross—Where All Attributes Meet

**Core Truth:** The cross is where God's holiness, justice, wrath, love, mercy, and grace are perfectly united—not competing with one another, but converging in Christ.

### Supporting Passages

**Old Testament:**
- Isaiah 53:4-6 — The Suffering Servant bears the punishment for our sins
- Isaiah 53:10-11 — God's plan: "It was the Lord's will to crush him and cause him to suffer... he will see the light of life and be satisfied"

**New Testament:**
- Romans 5:6-11 — Christ died for the ungodly; His blood reconciles us to God
- 2 Corinthians 5:21 — "God made him who had no sin to be sin for us, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God"
- Hebrews 9:24-28 — Christ's sacrifice is once for all, perfect and complete
- 1 Peter 3:18 — "Christ also suffered once for sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, to bring you to God"

**Revelation:**
- Revelation 5:6-10 — The Lamb who was slain is worthy to receive power, wealth, wisdom, strength, honor, and glory
- Revelation 7:14-17 — Those who have washed their robes in the blood of the Lamb stand before God's throne
- Revelation 12:7-11 — The victory of Christ over Satan through His blood

### Discussion Questions
1. How does Christ's sacrifice demonstrate all of God's attributes working together?
2. Why is the cross the central image of both salvation and judgment?

## Theme 7: Christ as Both Lamb and Lion

**Core Truth:** The One who gave Himself to save sinners is the One who will ultimately judge every person and bring all evil to an end.

### Supporting Passages

**Old Testament:**
- Genesis 49:9-10 — Judah as a lion; the scepter belonging to the descendant (Messiah)
- Psalm 22 — The suffering psalmist (typifying Christ's crucifixion)
- Daniel 7:13-14 — The "Son of Man" given authority and an everlasting kingdom

**New Testament:**
- John 1:29 — "Look, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!"
- Revelation 1:8 — "I am the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the End"

**Revelation:**
- Revelation 5:5-6 — "See, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has triumphed... Then I saw a Lamb, looking as if it had been slain"
- Revelation 19:11-16 — The Lamb returns as the righteous judge and warrior
- Revelation 20:11-15 — The final judgment before the great white throne
- Revelation 21:22-24 — The Lamb is the light of the new creation; His kingdom is forever

### Discussion Questions
1. Why is it significant that the same person is both Lamb and Lion?
2. How does Christ's gentleness and Christ's judgment work together in your understanding?

---

## Theme 8: Revelation's Purpose—Hope for the Persecuted

**Core Truth:** Revelation was written to give persecuted Christians hope and assurance of God's ultimate victory.

### Supporting Passages

**New Testament:**
- Romans 8:28-39 — Nothing can separate us from God's love in Christ
- 1 Peter 1:3-9 — Living hope through Christ's resurrection; trials produce perseverance and proven character
- Hebrews 12:1-3 — Fix your eyes on Jesus, the author and perfecter of faith
- 2 Corinthians 4:16-18 — Our light and momentary troubles are achieving eternal glory

**Revelation:**
- Revelation 1:9 — John shares with his readers in "the suffering and kingdom and patient endurance that are ours in Jesus"
- Revelation 2:10 — "Be faithful, even to the point of death, and I will give you the crown of life"
- Revelation 3:10-11 — Promise of protection for those who persevere
- Revelation 7:9-17 — The great multitude from every nation, tribe, people, and language clothed in white robes
- Revelation 12:11 — "They triumphed over him by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony"
- Revelation 21:1-7 — God's promise: "I am making everything new... Whoever is victorious will inherit all this"

### Discussion Questions
1. How does understanding Revelation's original purpose help us apply it to our own trials?
2. What specific promises from Revelation give you hope today?

---

## Theme 9: The Invitation of Grace—Revelation's Final Word

**Core Truth:** Even as Revelation moves toward final judgment, the invitation of grace remains open until the end.

### Supporting Passages

**Old Testament:**
- Isaiah 45:22 — "Turn to me and be saved, all you ends of the earth"
- Jeremiah 29:13 — "You will seek me and find me when you seek me with all your heart"

**New Testament:**
- Matthew 11:28 — "Come to me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest"
- John 6:37 — "All that the Father gives me will come to me, and whoever comes to me I will never drive away"
- Revelation 22:17 — "Let the one who is thirsty come; let the one who desires take the water of life freely"

**Revelation:**
- Revelation 3:20 — "Here I am! I stand at the door and knock. If anyone hears my voice and opens the door, I will come in"
- Revelation 22:12-14 — "My reward is with me, and I will give to each person according to what they have done. Blessed are those who wash their robes..."

### Discussion Questions
1. Why does Revelation end with an invitation rather than a warning?
2. How should this invitation shape the way Christians live and witness?

## Study Guide Format

### For Group Study:

1. **Read the passage together** (aloud, taking turns if possible)
2. **Identify the key theme** being studied
3. **Read the supporting verses** in order (OT → NT → Revelation)
4. **Discuss the discussion questions** as a group
5. **Apply to your life:** How does this truth change the way you live, trust, and witness?
6. **Pray together** for wisdom and transformation

### For Personal Study:

1. Read through each theme carefully
2. Look up the passages in your Bible (don't just read them here)
3. Write down insights, questions, or connections you make
4. Reflect on the discussion questions in your journal
5. Consider how this theme affects your relationship with God
